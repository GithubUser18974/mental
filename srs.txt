# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Purpose

This document defines the functional and non-functional requirements for an anonymous mental health community and personal wellness tracking application.

The platform will allow users to:
- Share personal mental health experiences anonymously.
- Connect and interact with people experiencing similar challenges.
- Maintain private mental health profiles.
- Track moods, symptoms, emotions, sleep and personal experiences.
- Record medications, dosages, treatment schedules and medication experiences.
- Maintain private journals.
- Monitor personal progress over time.

The system must prioritize privacy, anonymity between community members, security, user safety, accessibility and scalability.

## 1.2 Product Vision

Create a digital environment where people can express themselves without fear of judgment, connect with others who understand their experiences, and better understand their personal mental health journey.

The platform is not intended to diagnose, treat or replace professional mental health services.

## 1.3 Objectives

1. Provide a safe anonymous social environment.
2. Encourage healthy peer-to-peer support.
3. Allow individuals to monitor their mental health over time.
4. Provide private medication and treatment tracking.
5. Encourage self-awareness through personal insights.
6. Protect sensitive personal and health information.
7. Build a scalable technical foundation for future features.

## 1.4 Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Anonymous Profile | Public identity that does not expose real identity |
| Mood Entry | Daily emotional status record |
| Symptom Entry | Record of experienced symptoms |
| Medication Log | Record of medication intake |
| Journal Entry | Private personal writing |
| Community Post | Content shared publicly or within the community |
| Peer Support | Emotional support between community members |
| Moderation | Review and management of inappropriate content |
| Crisis Content | Content indicating potential immediate risk of harm |

# 3. User Roles and Permissions

The system shall support the following user roles.

## 3.1 Guest User

A guest user can:
- View introductory information.
- Explore public application information.
- View onboarding screens.
- Register or log in.

A guest cannot interact with community content or access personal tracking features.

## 3.2 Registered User

A registered user can:
- Create and manage an anonymous profile.
- Publish posts.
- Comment on posts.
- React to content.
- Follow other users.
- Save posts.
- Track moods and symptoms.
- Manage medications.
- Create private journal entries.
- View personal insights.
- Report inappropriate content.
- Block other users.
- Manage privacy settings.

## 3.3 Moderator

A moderator can:
- Review reported content.
- Hide or remove inappropriate content.
- Restrict users from publishing.
- Suspend accounts according to moderation policies.
- Review moderation history.
- Escalate critical safety incidents.

Moderators must not have unrestricted access to private journals or personal medication records.

## 3.4 Administrator

An administrator can:
- Manage application users.
- Manage community categories and topics.
- Manage moderation roles.
- Configure application settings.
- Manage notification templates.
- Review system reports and analytics.
- Manage crisis-support resources.
- Manage moderation policies.

Administrative access to sensitive information must be restricted, authorized and audited.

# 4. Functional Requirements

## 4.1 Authentication and Account Management

### FR-AUTH-001: User Registration

The system shall allow users to create accounts using supported authentication methods.

Supported methods:
- Email and password.
- Phone number and OTP (optional).
- Apple Sign-In.
- Google Sign-In.

The system shall generate a unique internal user identifier.

### FR-AUTH-002: Anonymous Identity

The system shall generate a pseudonymous profile for every registered user.

Requirements:
- Automatically generated username.
- Generated avatar.
- Optional custom username.
- Optional biography.
- Optional profile interests.
- Ability to change username subject to availability and abuse-prevention rules.

The public identity must not expose the user's real name, email address, phone number or authentication credentials.

**Important:** Anonymous means anonymous to other community members. The platform may still process account identifiers and security logs. The privacy policy must explain this distinction.

### FR-AUTH-003: Account Security

The system shall:
- Support secure authentication.
- Hash passwords using a secure password hashing algorithm.
- Support password recovery.
- Support session expiration.
- Support logout from all devices.
- Rate-limit authentication attempts.
- Protect against brute-force attacks.

### FR-AUTH-004: Account Deletion

Users shall be able to request account deletion.

The system shall:
- Explain the consequences before deletion.
- Remove or anonymize public profile information.
- Remove or anonymize community contributions according to the deletion policy.
- Delete personal health records and journals according to applicable retention requirements.
- Revoke active authentication sessions.
- Record the deletion request status.

The system must disclose any legally required retention exceptions.

### FR-ONB-001: Welcome Screens

The application shall provide onboarding screens explaining:

- Anonymous community concept.

### FR-ONB-002: Profile Setup

### FR-ONB-003: Privacy Preferences

## 4.3 Community Module

### FR-COM-001: Community Feed

The application shall provide a feed containing community publications.

The feed shall support:
- Recent posts.
- Popular posts.
- Posts from followed users.
- Topic-based filtering.
- Search and discovery.

Feed ranking must avoid systematically promoting harmful, sensational or crisis-related content.

### FR-COM-002: Create Post

Users shall be able to publish anonymous posts.

Supported content:
- Text.
- Images (optional).
- Hashtags.
- Topics and categories.
- Content warnings.
- Optional comments.

Each post shall contain:
- Post ID.
- Author profile ID.
- Content.
- Category.
- Created date.
- Updated date.
- Moderation status.
- Visibility status.

### FR-COM-003: Post Categories

The platform shall provide the following initial categories:

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| My Story | Personal mental health experiences |
| Need Support | Seeking emotional support |
| Daily Thoughts | Sharing daily feelings |
| Medication Experience | Personal medication experiences |
| Recovery Journey | Sharing personal progress |
| Questions | Asking community questions |
| Mental Wellness | General wellness discussions |

Administrators shall be able to add, edit and deactivate categories.

### FR-COM-004: Comments

Users shall be able to:
- Comment on posts.
- Reply to comments.
- Edit their own comments.
- Delete their own comments.
- Report comments.

Comment visibility shall follow post visibility and moderation rules.

### FR-COM-005: Reactions

The application shall provide supportive reactions rather than relying only on conventional likes.

Initial reactions:
- I understand you.
- Sending support.
- You're not alone.
- Proud of you.
- Thank you for sharing.

Users can add or remove reactions.

A user shall have a maximum of one active reaction type per post, unless product policy changes.

### FR-COM-006: Follow Users

Users shall be able to:
- Follow anonymous profiles.
- Unfollow profiles.
- View followed profiles.
- Receive notifications for selected followed-user activities.

### FR-COM-007: Save Posts

Users shall be able to bookmark posts privately.

Saved posts must never appear publicly on their profile.

### FR-COM-008: Search and Discovery

The application shall support searching by:
- Topics.
- Hashtags.
- Categories.
- Anonymous usernames.
- Post content.

Search results must respect moderation, blocking and visibility rules.

### FR-COM-009: Blocking

Users shall be able to block other users.

Blocking shall:
- Prevent the blocked user from interacting with the blocker.
- Hide relevant content between the two users.
- Prevent follow relationships.
- Restrict direct communication if introduced in the future.

### FR-COM-010: Reporting

Users shall be able to report:
- Posts.
- Comments.
- Profiles.
- Harassment.
- Dangerous medical advice.
- Spam.
- Self-harm encouragement.
- Suicidal content.
- Other policy violations.

Reports shall be sent to the moderation system.

## 4.4 Anonymous User Profile

### FR-PRO-001: Profile Information

Each user shall have a public anonymous profile containing:

- Anonymous username.
- Avatar.
- Biography.
- Joined date.
- Public posts.
- Public community activity.
- Interests and topics.
- Optional achievements.

### FR-PRO-002: Private Profile Information

The following information shall remain private:

- Real identity.
- Email and phone number.
- Medication records.
- Mood history.
- Symptom history.
- Journal entries.
- Private notes.
- Health insights.

### FR-PRO-003: Profile Statistics

The system may display:
- Number of public posts.
- Number of community contributions.
- Number of followers.
- Number of following profiles.
- Earned community achievements.

No health-related metrics shall be exposed publicly by default.

## 4.5 Mental Health Tracking

### FR-TRACK-001: Daily Check-in

Users shall be able to submit daily mental health check-ins.

Each check-in shall support:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Mood | Overall emotional state |
| Mood score | Scale from 1 to 10 |
| Anxiety | Scale from 1 to 10 |
| Stress | Scale from 1 to 10 |
| Energy | Scale from 1 to 10 |
| Sleep quality | Scale from 1 to 10 |
| Sleep duration | Hours |
| Notes | Optional private description |
| Date | Check-in date |

Users may skip any optional field.

### FR-TRACK-002: Mood Selection

The application shall provide mood options such as:

- Very sad.
- Sad.
- Neutral.
- Good.
- Very good.

Users shall be able to select an emotional state and optionally provide additional details.

### FR-TRACK-003: Symptom Tracking

Users shall be able to record symptoms using configurable symptom categories.

Examples:
- Anxiety.
- Low energy.
- Difficulty concentrating.
- Irritability.
- Sleep disturbances.
- Loss of motivation.
- Restlessness.
- Sadness.

Each symptom entry may contain:
- Symptom type.
- Severity (1–10).
- Date and time.
- Duration.
- Optional private notes.

The symptom list shall be configurable by the application administrator.

### FR-TRACK-004: Mood History

The system shall display historical mood records using:

- Daily view.
- Weekly view.
- Monthly view.
- Calendar view.
- Graphical trends.

### FR-TRACK-005: Personal Insights

The system shall generate non-diagnostic observations based on user-recorded data.

Examples:
- Mood trends during the last 30 days.
- Frequently recorded emotions.
- Sleep and mood patterns.
- Stress trends.
- Personal improvement milestones.

Insights must not claim medical causation or provide a diagnosis.

### FR-TRACK-006: Edit and Delete Entries

Users shall be able to edit and delete their personal tracking entries.

All personal tracking records must remain private unless the user explicitly shares selected information.

## 4.6 Medication Management

### FR-MED-001: Add Medication

Users shall be able to add medication records.

Medication fields:

| Field | Required |
|---|---|
| Medication name | Yes |
| Dosage | Yes |
| Dosage unit | Yes |
| Frequency | Yes |
| Start date | Yes |
| End date | No |
| Instructions | No |
| Prescribing clinician | No |
| Reminder preference | No |
| Personal notes | No |

Medication entries are user-maintained records, not prescriptions.

### FR-MED-002: Medication Schedule

The application shall support medication schedules.

Supported scheduling options:
- Once daily.
- Multiple times per day.
- Specific days of the week.
- Custom schedules.
- Temporary schedules.

### FR-MED-003: Medication Reminders

Users shall be able to configure reminders.

The system shall:
- Schedule local notifications.
- Allow reminders to be enabled or disabled.
- Support timezone changes.
- Avoid notifying users after medication records have been discontinued.
- Avoid exposing medication names in notification previews by default.

### FR-MED-004: Medication Intake Logging

Users shall be able to record medication intake.

Supported statuses:
- Taken.
- Skipped.
- Missed.
- Delayed.

Each log shall include:
- Medication ID.
- Scheduled time.
- Actual intake time (if taken).
- Status.
- Optional notes.

### FR-MED-005: Medication Experience

Users shall be able to privately record:
- Perceived benefits.
- Side effects.
- Physical or emotional changes.
- General experience.
- Date of observation.

### FR-MED-006: Medication History

The application shall display:
- Current medications.
- Past medications.
- Medication schedules.
- Intake history.
- Recorded experiences.

The application must not recommend changing dosage, stopping medication or starting treatment based on community posts or automated insights.

### FR-MED-007: Sharing Medication Experiences

Users may voluntarily create community posts based on their medication experiences.

The application must require an explicit publishing action and must not automatically expose private medication records.

## 4.7 Private Journal

### FR-JOURNAL-001: Create Journal Entry

Users shall be able to create private journal entries.

Each entry may include:
- Title.
- Content.
- Mood.
- Related tags.
- Date.
- Optional attachments.

### FR-JOURNAL-002: Journal Management

Users shall be able to:
- Create entries.
- Edit entries.
- Delete entries.
- Search entries.
- Filter by date.
- Filter by mood.
- View journal history.

### FR-JOURNAL-003: Journal Privacy

Journal entries shall be private by default.

They shall not be indexed by community search or included in public profiles.

### FR-JOURNAL-004: Share Journal Entry

Users may explicitly convert a journal entry into a community post.

Before publishing, the application shall:
- Show the complete content.
- Allow editing.
- Explain that the content will become visible to the selected audience.
- Require confirmation.

## 4.8 Personal Progress Dashboard

### FR-PROGRESS-001: Dashboard

The application shall provide a personal dashboard containing:

- Current mood.
- Recent mood history.
- Weekly and monthly trends.
- Symptom history.
- Sleep history.
- Medication adherence records.
- Recent journal entries.
- Personal milestones.

### FR-PROGRESS-002: Milestones

The application may recognize milestones such as:
- First check-in.
- Seven consecutive days of tracking.
- First month of journaling.
- Personal goals achieved.

Milestones shall not imply that users have recovered or that their condition has improved medically.

### FR-PROGRESS-003: Data Visualization

The system shall provide clear charts and summaries.

Users shall be able to filter historical data by date range.

## 4.9 Notifications

### FR-NOTIF-001: Notification Types

The system shall support:

| Notification | Example |
|---|---|
| Community reaction | Someone understood your story |
| Comment | Someone commented on your post |
| Follow | Someone followed your profile |
| Medication reminder | A scheduled reminder |
| Daily check-in | Time for your daily check-in |
| Personal milestone | You completed seven check-ins |
| Moderation | Update on a reported post |

### FR-NOTIF-002: Notification Preferences

Users shall be able to:
- Enable or disable notification categories.
- Configure reminder times.
- Configure quiet hours.
- Disable sensitive notification previews.

Notifications must not expose sensitive information on a locked device.

## 4.10 Crisis Support and Community Safety

### FR-SAFETY-001: Content Safety

The system shall detect and route potentially dangerous content for appropriate safety handling.

Potential content categories:
- Suicidal ideation.
- Self-harm.
- Encouragement of self-harm.
- Medication misuse.
- Threats or harassment.
- Dangerous medical instructions.

Automated detection must not be the sole basis for deciding that a user is safe or for making irreversible account decisions.

### FR-SAFETY-002: Crisis Support

When content indicates possible immediate danger, the application shall display a supportive intervention.

The intervention should:
- Acknowledge the user's distress.
- Encourage contacting emergency services or a trusted person when immediate danger exists.
- Display verified local crisis and emergency resources.
- Offer an appropriate reporting or escalation pathway.
- Avoid judgmental language.

Crisis resources must be verified and configurable by administrators for supported countries.

### FR-SAFETY-003: Moderation

Moderators shall be able to review flagged content.

The system shall maintain:
- Report status.
- Moderation decision.
- Moderator ID.
- Action date.
- Reason for action.
- Audit history.

### FR-SAFETY-004: Community Guidelines

The application shall prohibit:
- Harassment.
- Bullying.
- Discrimination.
- Sharing another user's identity without consent.
- Dangerous medical instructions.
- Encouraging self-harm.
- Promoting suicide.
- Selling prescription medication.
- Exploitation of vulnerable members.

The backend shall follow a modular monolith architecture.

Recommended modules:

1. Authentication.
2. Identity and Profiles.
3. Community.
4. Content Moderation.
5. Mental Health Tracking.
6. Medication Management.
7. Journaling.
8. Notifications.
9. Reporting and Analytics.
10. Administration.

Each module should have clearly defined responsibilities and boundaries.

The mobile application shall follow Clean Architecture with feature-based organization.

Recommended Flutter layers:

- Presentation.
- Application / Providers.
- Domain.
- Data.

Business logic must not be implemented directly inside UI widgets.

## 6.3 Backend Architecture Principles

- RESTful API conventions.
- Service-oriented business logic.
- Repository abstraction where useful.
- Form Request validation.
- Resource-based API responses.
- Dependency injection.
- Centralized exception handling.
- Database transactions for critical operations.
- Queue-based background jobs.
- Structured logging.
- Role-based authorization policies.

Microservices are not required for the initial release.

## 7.7 Database Design Rules

- All entities shall have primary keys.
- Foreign keys shall enforce relational integrity.
- Sensitive information shall have restricted access.
- Timestamps shall be stored consistently in UTC.
- User-facing dates shall be converted using the user's timezone.
- Appropriate database indexes shall be created.
- Soft deletion shall be used only where justified by retention and privacy requirements.
- Medication schedules shall support timezone and daylight-saving changes.
- Private health records shall never be joined into public community responses.

```json
    "mood": "good",

    "recorded_at": "2026-11-12T10:00:00Z"
  }
}
```

Error response:

```json
{
  "success": false,
  "message": "Validation failed",
  "errors": {
    "score": [
      "The score must be between 1 and 10."
    ]
  }
}
```

All private endpoints must verify ownership and authorization, not merely the existence of the requested record.

# 9. Non-Functional Requirements

## 9.1 Performance

- Standard API responses should target less than 500 ms at the 95th percentile under the defined normal operating load.
- Feed pagination shall avoid loading the entire dataset.
- Images shall be optimized and compressed.
- Database queries shall use appropriate indexing.
- Background jobs shall handle non-critical processing.
- Large analytics calculations shall not block user requests.

## 9.2 Scalability

The system shall support:
- Horizontal backend scaling.
- Database connection pooling.
- Redis caching.
- Queue workers.
- Object storage.
- CDN-based media delivery.
- Future extraction of independent modules into services.

## 9.3 Security

The system shall implement:

- HTTPS for all network communication.
- Secure password hashing using Argon2id or an appropriately configured bcrypt implementation.
- Secure token storage on mobile devices.
- Role-based access control.
- Resource-level ownership authorization.
- Rate limiting.
- Input validation and sanitization.
- SQL injection protection.
- Protection against XSS and CSRF where applicable.
- Secure file upload validation.
- Encryption of sensitive data at rest.
- Encryption of backups.
- Secret management outside source code.
- Security audit logs.
- Restricted administrative access.

Sensitive health information should be encrypted using a managed encryption key system, with key rotation and access auditing.

The system should undergo security testing before public release.

## 9.4 Privacy

Privacy is a critical system requirement.

The system shall:
- Keep health data private by default.
- Separate authentication identity from anonymous community identity.
- Prevent accidental exposure of personal records.
- Avoid putting health data into analytics or crash-reporting payloads.
- Provide data export and deletion mechanisms.
- Minimize collected personal information.
- Explain data collection and retention.
- Obtain explicit consent for optional sensitive processing.

The product must undergo a privacy and legal review against applicable requirements, including Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law and relevant requirements in any other launch market.

## 9.5 Availability

Initial production availability target: 99.5% monthly, excluding scheduled maintenance.

The infrastructure shall support:
- Automated backups.
- Database recovery procedures.
- Monitoring.
- Error tracking.
- Service health checks.
- Incident response procedures.

## 9.6 Usability

- Support Arabic and English.
- Support RTL and LTR layouts.
- Provide accessible typography.
- Support light and dark modes.
- Follow accessibility guidelines.
- Use empathetic and non-judgmental language.
- Avoid clinical or stigmatizing interface language.

## 9.7 Compatibility

The mobile application shall support:
- iOS.
- Android.
- Multiple screen sizes.
- Different screen densities.
- Modern OS versions defined in the release compatibility policy.

# 17. Important Product and Safety Constraints

The following constraints are mandatory design considerations:

1. The application is a mental wellness and peer-support platform, not a medical diagnosis or treatment system.
2. The application shall not recommend starting, stopping or changing prescribed medication.
3. Community medication experiences must be clearly labeled as personal experiences.
4. Private health information shall never be automatically shared with the community.
5. Crisis-support workflows must be designed with qualified mental health and safety professionals.
6. The product must undergo legal, privacy and security review before public launch.
7. User anonymity must be accurately described and must not be marketed as absolute technical anonymity.
8. The application shall not use sensitive health information for advertising or unrelated profiling.
